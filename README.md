# The AI Story Bank

A [Rational Optimist Society](https://rationaloptimistsociety.com) project.

**Live: https://stories.rationaloptimistsociety.com**

142 true, sourced stories of artificial intelligence doing something good — curing
disease, catching cancer early, restoring lost abilities, teaching children,
protecting the planet.

Nuclear power and supersonic flight were both effectively banned for fifty years,
not because they didn't work, but because of the stories told about them. In 1964
the FAA dropped 1,253 sonic booms on Oklahoma City to see whether Americans could
live with supersonic flight. Nearly 73% said they could. About 3% objected
strongly — and that 3% was enough. This project exists because AI is on the same
path, and the counter-argument has to be concrete.

## The three rules

1. **It happened.** No demos, no promises, no "coming soon."
2. **It's measured.** A number, with a stated baseline.
3. **It has receipts.** 381 primary sources — papers, regulatory filings,
   government records — not press releases.

## We publish our own counter-evidence

A bank that only carries good news is propaganda. This one includes the AI
stethoscope trial that failed because 80% of GP practices never used the device;
the randomised trial where doctors given GPT-4 did no better than doctors without
it; and the school whose own homeschool arm learned at 1x while its campus arm hit
2.6x. 108 of the 142 stories carry a published caveat naming their limits.

Several widely circulated claims were investigated and **rejected** — including a
much-shared story about a child saved by a drone-delivered antivenom flight, whose
delivery number appears nowhere and whose central detail contradicts the
operator's own published figures. Where a number was corrected, the story says so.
